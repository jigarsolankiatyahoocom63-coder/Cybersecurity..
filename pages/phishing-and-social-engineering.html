Online Threats

Phishing and Social Engineering

Phishing attacks are designed to manipulate your decision-making rather than exploit a technical flaw. Attackers often create messages that seem urgent, familiar, or important enough to trigger a quick reply.

Examples include fake bank messages, delivery updates, password reset alerts, and job offers. These messages may direct you to a fake website or request sensitive information.

Signs of a phishing attempt

  • Urgent wording like “act now” or “your account is at risk”.
  • Unusual sender addresses or fake domains.
  • Links that are slightly different from the real website name.
  • Requests for password, OTP, or payment information.

Safer response

  • Open the official website yourself rather than clicking the message link.
  • Check the domain carefully before entering information.
  • Report suspicious messages instead of forwarding them widely.

Spot the phishing message

Fake message: “Your bank account has been frozen. Click here now to confirm your identity and fix the problem.”

Why this is dangerous: It uses urgency, fear, and a direct action request. A safer response is to visit the bank’s official website or app directly and check the alert there.

Key takeaways

  • Phishing is a trust attack, not just a technical attack.
  • Urgency and fear are warning signs.
  • Verification through official channels is always safer than acting on the message itself.
