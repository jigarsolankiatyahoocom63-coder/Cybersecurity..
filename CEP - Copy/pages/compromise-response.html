What to Do After an Account or Data Compromise

If you suspect a compromise, do not wait for it to worsen. A quick, organised response reduces damage and prevents the attacker from continuing access.

Accounts become compromised through reused passwords, phishing attacks, malicious links, malware, or unsafe recovery methods. The first goal is to stop the attacker’s access and then assess what else may be at risk.

Step-by-step response

  1. Lock the account and change the password immediately.
  2. Enable or update 2FA on the account.
  3. Review recent logins, security alerts, and linked devices.
  4. Check email, banking, and social accounts for unusual messages or payments.
  5. Review other accounts that use the same or similar passwords.

If the compromise is serious

Contact the service provider, freeze or monitor payment methods, and document the date, what was changed, and any suspicious contacts.

Key takeaways

  • Immediate action limits the damage.
  • Check linked services and shared passwords after a breach.
  • Review device security to reduce the chance of repeat compromise.
