Password Hygiene

Creating Strong Passwords

A strong password is not simply a long password. It should also be difficult to guess, avoid common words or patterns, and be unique to each account.

Strong examples are often passphrases made from unrelated words, numbers, and symbols such as “Pineapple#River9!Clock” or “Cedar!Mango@Orbit7”. These are memorable to the user but difficult for attackers to predict.

Do

  • Use 12 or more characters when possible.
  • Mix upper and lower case, numbers, and symbols.
  • Use a memorable passphrase rather than a simple word.
  • Keep each account unique.

Don’t

  • Avoid names, birthdays, sports teams, and pet names.
  • Avoid “password”, “welcome”, or repeated patterns.
  • Avoid writing passwords in notebooks or unclear notes.

Key takeaways

  • Use unique passwords for every important account.
  • Longer passphrases are stronger and easier to remember.
  • Use password managers to avoid memorising too many weak passwords.
